# Inventory Upgrade — Spare Bag

![Gabby — lost girl in Elderfield Town who gives the Spare Bag](https://pub-569323f832f84244a5766b065936dc37.r2.dev/LucasGuide/Welcome-to-Elderfield-NPC-Gabby-LucasGuide.jpg)

Inventory space in Welcome to Elderfield is the quiet bottleneck nobody warns you about. You pick up herbs, mushrooms, ritual tomes, keys, and crafting junk faster than you can spend them, and suddenly you're standing in a dungeon deciding which item gets abandoned.

The Spare Bag is the cheapest fix in the game: one conversation with a lost girl in Elderfield Town, zero gold, and a permanent +1 to your inventory. This guide covers exactly where she is, what to do, and the small mistakes that cost people the upgrade.

| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Find the lost girl in Elderfield Town | She's standing around, waiting to be talked to |
| 2 | Talk to her | She hands you the Spare Bag |
| 3 | Use the Spare Bag immediately | Inventory space permanently increases by 1 |

- **Prerequisite:** Talk to the lost girl in town — no quest flag, no item required.
- **Reward:** Spare Bag (inventory +1), permanent.
- **Cost:** Nothing. It's free.

> **Note:** The +1 is permanent and applies to your character, not the bag item. Once used, the bag is consumed and the slot is yours forever.

### Why This Upgrade Matters More Than It Looks

One slot sounds like a rounding error. It isn't, and here's the design reason: Elderfield's progression is built around hoarding small, stackable resources — herbs, mushrooms, ore, ritual tomes, keys, and food ingredients all compete for the same limited space.

When I mapped my early route, the friction wasn't combat. It was the constant inventory shuffle before every dungeon run. One extra slot doesn't solve that alone, but it's the first domino. Grab it before you start farming anything seriously.

If you're already collecting materials, pair this with [foraging and resource collection](/welcome-to-elderfield/foraging-and-resource-collection) so you're not making two trips for the same herbs.

### Department store — paid expansions

![General store — bag expansions and equipment boxes](https://pub-569323f832f84244a5766b065936dc37.r2.dev/LucasGuide/Welcome-to-Elderfield-General-Store-LucasGuide.jpg)

After the free Spare Bag, the **department store** sells:

- **Inventory expansions** — stack these when gold allows
- **Random equipment boxes** — gamble gear; I buy several early for cheap stat bumps before mall mine fights

Still hit the lost girl first — zero cost, permanent +1.

### How to Find the Lost Girl in Elderfield Town

She's in Elderfield Town, and she is not hidden behind a puzzle, a locked door, or a boss. That's the whole point — this is an early, low-effort upgrade.

What to do:

1. Enter Elderfield Town and walk the main open area rather than the building interiors.
2. Look for the girl standing on her own — she reads as "lost," not as a vendor or quest-giver with a marker.
3. Approach and talk to her. No dialogue choice matters here; the Spare Bag is given regardless.

> **Tip:** If you can't spot her on your first pass, do a slow loop of the town's central path before checking side buildings. She's easy to walk past when you're beelining toward a shop.

### Claiming and Using the Spare Bag

This is where people fumble it. The Spare Bag is an item, not an automatic upgrade — you have to open your inventory and use it.

1. Finish the conversation with the girl.
2. Open your inventory and locate the **Spare Bag**.
3. Use it immediately. Don't bank it "for later."

> **Warning:** If you're already at a full inventory when you receive it, clear one slot first so the bag doesn't get awkwardly shuffled around your bag space. Use it the moment you get it.

Once used, the bag disappears and your slot count goes up by one. There's no animation worth waiting for, no confirmation screen that matters — just check your inventory capacity afterward to confirm.

### What to Do With the Extra Slot

A single slot is best spent on something you'd otherwise drop mid-run. My rule: dedicate it to consumables you actually use, not vendor trash.

| Priority | Item Type | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Healing food (e.g. Vegetable Soup) | You always want one more heal than you think |

| 2 | Ritual tomes you haven't read yet | Easy to forget, annoying to re-find |
| 3 | Keys and quest items | Never drop these — they're the worst thing to lose |

If you're cooking your own heals, this slot pays for itself fast. See [early game cooking](/welcome-to-elderfield/cooking-and-crafting) for the most efficient early heal, and [welcome to elderfield cooking and recipes](/welcome-to-elderfield/cooking-and-crafting) if you want the full recipe list.

- **Walking past her.** She has no glowing quest marker, so players assume she's scenery. She isn't.
- **Banking the bag.** The Spare Bag does nothing sitting in your inventory. Use it.
- **Assuming it stacks.** It doesn't. This is a one-time +1, not a repeatable farm.
- **Skipping it because "one slot is nothing."** Then complaining about inventory pressure two hours later.

> **Note:** This is a one-off reward from a specific NPC. Don't expect to find a second Spare Bag elsewhere in Elderfield Town.

### My Route Judgment

If I'm starting a fresh run, the Spare Bag is on my mandatory early checklist alongside the free movement and utility unlocks. It costs one conversation.

I grab it before I touch any serious gathering loop, because every resource run afterward benefits from the extra space.

 If you're optimizing your early game, this sits right next to [warp in welcome to elderfield](/welcome-to-elderfield/ritual-skills-reference) in terms of value-per-minute — free, permanent, and easy to miss.

Get the bag, use the bag, then go farm without the inventory shuffle. That's the whole play.

### Final Thoughts

The Spare Bag isn't a flashy reward, and that's exactly why it's worth grabbing early. It removes friction from everything you do afterward — gathering, cooking, dungeon diving, and crafting.

Talk to the lost girl, use the bag the second you get it, and treat that extra slot as your dedicated consumables space. Small upgrade, permanent payoff.

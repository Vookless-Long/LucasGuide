# Beginner's Tips

I went into Welcome to Elderfield expecting a cozy farming loop and got ambushed by a full RPG with missable skills, weekly god worship, and a first dungeon that half the playerbase ignores because it feels like late-game content. It isn't.

This is the starter roadmap I wish someone had handed me: what to do in your **first in-game month** so nothing permanently missable slips through your fingers. Brand new and wasting seasons? Start here. Already cleared the mall and figured out the church? Skip to the sections you haven't nailed yet.

The core idea is simple. Almost nothing is gated at the start, so you should be **exploring aggressively from hour one** instead of tidying your farm. The game rewards curiosity constantly and punishes hanging back — not by deleting your save, but by making you do everything in a less efficient order.

For the full linear story route, see the [main walkthrough](/welcome-to-elderfield). This page is the **systems cheat sheet** I keep open on a second monitor during week one.

## Explore everything the moment you can

When you first start, almost everything is available right away. There is no tiny starting zone holding you back. You can head into the sewers, wander through the mall, poke around the creepy Greystone estate, or ignore all of that and focus on your farm and [fishing](/welcome-to-elderfield/fishing).

Walk into every building and every weird door you find. If enemies are too strong, leave and come back after you've leveled up. Don't let one tough fight convince you an entire area isn't worth visiting yet. The game signals when something is out of your depth, usually through enemy difficulty, but that doesn't mean you should avoid the whole zone forever.

> **Tip:** Enemy difficulty is a "come back later" sign, not a "never go here" sign.

Click on everything, too. A lot of items look like plain terrain at first glance but turn out to be useful crafting materials. The art style is unfamiliar early on — I walked past half my first forage route because I didn't recognize pickups yet. See [foraging and resource collection](/welcome-to-elderfield/foraging-and-resource-collection) once you know what to hunt.

## The mall is your first dungeon, not late-game content

The game subtly pushes you toward the mall, which is the **first dungeon**. You get a quest requiring you to go there, but it is easy to ignore thinking it's late game. The tell is this: pretty much every NPC you talk to starts handing you side quests that all point toward the same location. Once you notice that pattern, go there.

The mall is required content — you must clear it to progress the main quest and story. It is **not** overleveled. Enemies there can be one-hit. Get there as early as you reasonably can, then explore as much of it as possible. Talk to every NPC inside. Check every corner, every room, every nook you can physically get into.

### Don't miss Warp at Ultra Video

You can miss a crucial skill inside the mall: **[Warp](/welcome-to-elderfield/ritual-skills-reference)**, found in **Ultra Video** on Mall F1. Talk to the Shopper on the far right by the SALE display — the ritual is free.

![Shopper in Ultra Video — free Warp ritual](https://pub-569323f832f84244a5766b065936dc37.r2.dev/LucasGuide/Welcome-to-Elderfield-NPC-Shopper-LucasGuide.jpg)

**NPC Shopper**

Once you have Warp, you can teleport straight back to your home from basically anywhere on the map, within about a five-minute walk of home. The map is big enough that a five-minute walk is a real distance. Warp costs nothing and saves an enormous amount of time — I treat it as mandatory before I do any serious farming or foraging loops.

Near the end of the mall area, you'll find the **mall mines**. Get in there and start collecting **copper and iron** as soon as you are able, because you will need both constantly for crafting essential items and for upgrading your tools. The earlier you have a steady supply, the smoother the rest of your progression will go. Tool upgrades live in [cooking and crafting](/welcome-to-elderfield/cooking-and-crafting).

Also grab [mall vending machine tomes](/welcome-to-elderfield/mall-vending-machine-tomes) when you have spare gold — **Lesser Mending** alone pays for itself fast.

## Don't walk past Molly's workshop

On the path leading to your farm, there is a small mine and a workshop run by an NPC named **Molly**. She gives you a quest that sends you off to mine for the first time, and as part of that quest she hands you the **starter pickaxe**. This is literally on the way to the farm, which you visit every day.

![Molly at her workshop forge](https://pub-569323f832f84244a5766b065936dc37.r2.dev/LucasGuide/Welcome-to-Elderfield-NPC-Molly-Romance-LucasGuide.jpg)

**NPC Molly**

Skipping Molly does not break anything or block progress, but if you want to check off quests in the intended order and get your tools sorted from the start, stop at her workshop on your way to Hans Farm. The hub's [Mines — Molly's Workshop](/welcome-to-elderfield#mines--mollys-workshop) section covers the furnace spike once you bring copper ore.

## Old god doors and why the church matters early

As you explore, you'll come across doors scattered around the map, each marked with a different symbol, each telling you that you need to be a follower of a specific old god to enter. These are **not** late-game mechanics that unlock naturally through the main story.

You can visit the **church every Sunday** and choose to pray to an old god, whichever one you like. Doing this grants you that god's gifts and perks for the week, and it also unlocks the locked doors tied to that specific god — which usually contain treasure boxes filled with loot.

![Father Desmond — choose your deity on Sundays at church](https://pub-569323f832f84244a5766b065936dc37.r2.dev/LucasGuide/Welcome-to-Elderfield-Father-Desmond-LucasGuide.jpg)

**Father Desmond**

You can only pick **one god at a time**, but you are free to switch which one you follow every week. Nice way to experiment with your build:

- One god can boost your physical attack
- One god can give you more magical damage or more resistance

Finding the church earlier gives you access to these perks and unlocks those loot doors way sooner — far more useful in the early game. Room-by-room routes: [god rooms](/welcome-to-elderfield/god-rooms).

While prioritizing places to visit early, add the **library** to that list. Heading there as soon as you can gets you started on the **tower quest** tied to your farm. That quest line runs in the background for a while and takes a lot of time to progress, so starting it early means you are further along by the time it becomes relevant. See [Stone Tower Mystery](/welcome-to-elderfield#stone-tower-mystery) on the hub.

## Give your two weekly gifts, even blindly

For roughly the first month of playing, it is easy to ignore the other townsfolk and give zero gifts, mostly because you don't know what items are valued or what you should keep versus safely gift. That is a mistake.

You are limited to giving only **two gifts per week to each character**, which means every week you skip is a week of relationship progress with that character you can never get back.

Once you start paying attention to what people like, gift giving becomes a lot more worthwhile. Use your two weekly gifts even if you don't feel warm to the characters yet — they get better. Even if you don't know what to give them, you can still make progress and learn what they dislike and hate. The game records everything and puts it in the social tab.

> **Tip:** Sunflower **seeds** (from harvesting sunflowers) are a common early trap — townsfolk hate them. Harvest flowers for more seeds to plant, not for gifting.

## Daily TV events are optional flavor

Every day, your TV shows a special event picked randomly. Some days it's a blood trail you can follow. Other days it's a phone number to call, a treasure to track down, or even just a dog that wants to play.

Most of these events lead toward **monster encounters** rather than treasure. The monsters you run into through these events tend to be significantly overleveled compared to where you will be when you first encounter them, and chasing them down usually means burning through resources for very little payoff.

You don't need to rush into interacting with every single daily event the moment it appears, because they appear again over and over. Treat them as optional flavor rather than something you have to engage with immediately. When you're ready for the serious moon stuff, see [moon events](/welcome-to-elderfield/moon-events).

There is also fun environmental stuff tied into this system. During the summer season, there is a daily event where standing outside under the sun actually damages you over time — you get **sun damage**.

## Seasons, farm plots, and the scarecrow rule

Seasons work differently here. Instead of the traditional spring, summer, autumn, winter format, there are four seasons: the season of rebirth, the season of the harvest, the season of the witch, and the season of death. Structurally they behave the same as expected: **28 days each**, with an event to mark the end of every season.

The big difference: most crops aren't locked to a single season. After more than 30 hours of play time, you can plant almost anything without worrying about a season ending and wiping out your crops. There are exceptions — some crops are still season specific — but the general rule is a lot more forgiving than other farming sims.

### Farm quirks worth knowing up front

**Dedicated plots:** When you first look at your farm, you'll notice a few plots that are already open and ready for planting, separate from the rest of your farm area. Regular seeds won't work in them. These are meant for **flower seeds and specialty stuff like fruit trees**, which you can buy from a shop in town. If your normal seeds won't touch them, they aren't broken.

**Fast respawns:** Resources respawn faster on your farm. If your goal is to clear every piece of wood and every stone, those resources come back surprisingly quickly — pair this with [Warp](/welcome-to-elderfield/ritual-skills-reference) and bath loops for easy early XP.

**The scarecrow rule:** Do not plant your valuable crops before you have a scarecrow up, because the birds in this game are relentless and seem to specifically target the most valuable seeds. You can buy the starter scarecrow for really cheap — around **five gold** from the person you buy your crops from.

**Stay in place:** When sowing a plot or watering your crops, stay in place rather than moving around, because the game automatically selects the next tile within your range for you. This makes planting large plots much faster.

**Rain quirk:** If it's raining and you harvest a crop and then immediately plant something new in that same spot, the new plant doesn't get watered by the rain the same day. Don't assume a freshly planted crop is covered just because it's raining.

## Sell to the traveling merchant, not the regular vendors

There is a **traveling merchant** who shows up every weekend and offers to buy your crops for a noticeably better price than the regular NPCs in town. Each week they choose **two random crops** they are specifically interested in and offer more money for those items.

Hold off on selling everything to regular vendors and instead stockpile your crops throughout the week. Then check what the traveling merchant wants when they show up and sell those crops to them for the bonus.

## Sleeping, saving, and debuffs

Sleep isn't mandatory in this game, but it still matters. You can go without sleeping and keep playing, but you will slowly pick up **debuffs** the longer you go without rest — including [Heavy Eyelids](/welcome-to-elderfield/blessings-and-curses), which cuts EXP gain until you sleep.

You also don't need to sleep in order to **save your game** — you can save any time from your bed regardless of whether you are actually going to sleep. This means you are free to stay out for multiple days in a row without ever returning home. But every night you skip stacks another debuff on top of the last, and your character gets progressively more tired and weaker.

There is no strict day and night cycle in the sense of shops closing at certain hours. You can tackle your quests whenever you want, day or night.

**Sleeping does not heal you.** If you go to sleep with 10 HP, you wake up with 10 HP, or maybe less if you have an unlucky night — some things might attack you in your sleep or you might straight up die. You can't even sleep safely at low HP. Sometimes you might actually get some blessings during your sleep as well.

## Storage and healing — the two systems that trip up new players

Your backpack storage is limited, but you can slowly add to it. You can buy extra pouches from certain vendors around town, or you can get them as drops from enemies. Grab the free [Spare Bag](/welcome-to-elderfield/inventory-upgrade-spare-bag) from the lost girl in town early.

Your **home chest** has no limits at all. You also get a **fridge** specifically for storing food you plan to cook with, and that also has unlimited space.

The catch: if you put food items into your regular chest instead of the fridge, the game won't recognize them when you go to cook. **Keep your food in the fridge specifically**, or it won't show up as an option when you are cooking. You can also craft as many chests as you want, so storage space itself isn't really a bottleneck.

When crafting items, your chest storage **does** get counted — the game pulls materials from your chest automatically during crafting. But the **fridge isn't included** in that. Keep things like eyeballs, bugs, bones, or normal ingredients like salt in the fridge, and if you are missing an ingredient during crafting, check whether it is sitting in your fridge instead of your chest.

### Bath to heal, not sleep

Your HP doesn't refill just because you slept. This game handles healing almost like a Soulslike: you actually need to take a **bath** to heal, and doing so respawns enemies in the world. You can also cook and eat to heal, but showering is the fastest and easiest way that doesn't consume any materials.

Once a week, after you take a bath or after you die, the world **respawns its resources**. Make sure you are going out and collecting things like wood and forageable items regularly. Don't forget the mines either: if you clear out a mine completely, it will not simply respawn the next day — it is on this weekly cycle instead. Plan your resource runs with that timing in mind.

> **Warning:** Bathing wipes shrine buffs you just farmed. Only bathe when you need the heal or want a deliberate world reset — same rule as the [hub's first-night section](/welcome-to-elderfield#first-night--bath-and-sleep).

## Two small combat and crafting tricks

Since this is an RPG at its core, there are small mechanical tricks you can use to optimize your build. One example: a weapon that costs more mana per use but deals significantly more damage, while your healing skill also costs 50% more mana while that weapon is equipped. Quickly switch away from that weapon whenever you need to heal outside of combat, heal up, then switch straight back to the higher damage weapon afterwards.

The game is full of small mechanical interactions like this, so it is worth experimenting to find what setup works best for your specific build and play style — especially since resources are actually limited and you don't want to waste mana or materials unnecessarily. Save them for the [boss fights](/welcome-to-elderfield/boss-fights-and-elites).

When you are smelting iron bars or running anything at all through a crafting machine, the game doesn't display a running countdown for how much longer it will take. The only time you get this information is the moment you first place materials into the machine — the game will tell you something like "this will be done in 30 hours, 20 hours." After that initial message, it will not remind you again until the process is actually finished.

Pay close attention the first time you start a crafting job, because that is currently your only chance to know the timer before it disappears.

## First-month checklist

My condensed order for week one through four:

| Priority | Do this | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Explore mall + grab Warp | Missable, saves hours of walking |
| 2 | Molly pickaxe + mall mines | Copper/iron pipeline for tools |
| 3 | Library + Dayton | Tower quest starts in background |
| 4 | Church on first Sunday | God perks + locked door loot |
| 5 | Two gifts per NPC weekly | Missable relationship progress |
| 6 | Scarecrow before valuable seeds | Birds eat premium crops first |
| 7 | Food in fridge, mats in chest | Crafting pulls from wrong storage |
| 8 | Bath when hurt, sleep before day-2 moonrise | Heal vs EXP debuff — see [blessings and curses](/welcome-to-elderfield/blessings-and-curses) |

When you've cleared the mall and started pushing residential content, return to the [main walkthrough](/welcome-to-elderfield) for Adrian, Akira mysteries, and moon event prep.

# Ritual Skills Reference

Rituals are not optional. You start weak — hunt chamber doors, braziers, and vending tomes before mid-game fights.

![Rituals menu — combat and utility skills](https://pub-569323f832f84244a5766b065936dc37.r2.dev/LucasGuide/Welcome-to-Elderfield-Ritual-LucasGuide.jpg)

**Ritual**

## Overview

Rituals are the backbone of your combat and utility kit in Welcome to Elderfield, and the game does not hand them to you on a silver platter. You start with exactly two — Energy Blast and Guard — and everything else has to be found, bought, or earned through exploration and progression.

I have tested every ritual I could get my hands on across multiple runs, and the difference between a player who hunts rituals and one who ignores them is night and day. This overview breaks down what rituals are, how they work, and where to prioritize your search so you are not stuck spamming a 2 MP starter spell in the late game.

- **Everything else:** Found in the world, purchased from vendors, or earned through events and puzzles.
- **Utility rituals:** Some function outside battle from the Rituals menu — these are easy to miss if you never open that menu.
- **Combat rituals:** Used in battle, often costing MP, and scale with your stats and blessings.
- **Key sources:** Old ritual braziers, chamber doors in dungeons, vending machine tomes, and specific NPC interactions.

### What Are Rituals and Why They Matter

Rituals are special abilities that cover both combat and utility. In combat, they function like spells — you spend MP, you deal damage or apply effects. Outside combat, some rituals operate from the Rituals menu and handle traversal, defense, or resource management.

The trap I see new players fall into is treating rituals as optional. They are not. Your starting Energy Blast is a weak shot of energy — it works early, but it does not scale into the mid game. If you are still relying on it by the time you hit the Sewers or the Old Woods, you are making every fight harder than it needs to be.

> **Tip:** Open your Rituals menu regularly. Some rituals are passive or utility-based and will not appear in your battle command list. I missed Warp for an embarrassingly long time because I never checked the menu outside combat.

### The Two Rituals You Start With

| Ritual | MP Cost | Type | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Energy Blast | 2 MP | Combat | Weak shot of energy. Reliable early, falls off fast. |
| Guard | Varies | Combat | Defensive option. Essential for surviving early ambushes. |

Energy Blast is your bread and butter for the first hour. It is cheap, it hits, and it does not require any setup. But 2 MP for a weak shot means you are trading efficiency for accessibility. The moment you find a stronger blast variant, you should be swapping it in.

Guard is the one ritual I never remove from my active set. Defense in Elderfield is not optional — the game punishes greedy play, and Guard has saved more of my runs than any offensive ritual.

### How I Prioritize Ritual Hunting

When I enter a new area, rituals are one of three things I look for before anything else. Here is my priority order:

1. **Chamber doors and braziers** — These are the most common ritual sources. If you see an old ritual brazier, touch it. If you see a sealed chamber, open it.
2. **Vending machines and vendors** — Tomes and scrolls show up in shops and vending machines. Always check the Mall vending machine tomes stock.

3. **Event and puzzle rewards** — Some rituals are tied to specific encounters or puzzle solutions. These are easy to skip if you are rushing.
4. **NPC interactions** — A few rituals come from talking to the right person at the right time.

I clear the [pumpkin blast](/welcome-to-elderfield/ritual-skills-reference) chamber in the Old Woods before I do anything else in that zone because the ritual is too good to leave behind. Similarly, the [chaos thorns](/welcome-to-elderfield/ritual-skills-reference) in the Sewers is a priority grab — the Sewers are dangerous enough without skipping free power.

### Combat Rituals vs Utility Rituals

Not all rituals are built for fighting. This is the distinction that trips people up.

**Combat rituals** are used in battle and cost MP. They deal damage, apply status effects, or provide defensive options. Energy Blast, Dark Blast, Ghost Blast, and Chaos Thorns all fall here.

**Utility rituals** work outside battle from the Rituals menu. Warp is the classic example — it handles traversal and would be useless in a fight. Dark Shield is another one that blurs the line, offering defense that you can pre-cast.

> **Note:** If a ritual does not show up in your battle command list, check the Rituals menu. It is probably a utility ritual, not a bug.

### How Rituals Interact With Blessings and Curses

This is the part most overviews skip, and it matters. Ritual damage and effectiveness scale with your current stat modifiers, which means [welcome to elderfield blessings and curses](/welcome-to-elderfield/blessings-and-curses) directly affect how hard your rituals hit.

If you are stacking [welcome to elderfield common blessings](/welcome-to-elderfield/blessings-and-curses), your blast rituals get stronger. If you are carrying curses like the Dread debuff from the [strange moon event in welcome to elderfield](/welcome-to-elderfield/moon-events), your Ability stat drops and your rituals underperform.

> **Tip:** Before a boss fight, check your active blessings and curses. A single -10% Ability curse can turn a comfortable ritual kill into a resource-draining slog.

### My Rule for Ritual Upgrades

I do not hoard rituals. The moment I find a stronger blast variant, I swap it in and test it against the content I am currently clearing. If it kills faster for the same or less MP, it stays. If it does not, I keep the old one and move on.

The exception is utility rituals. Warp and Dark Shield stay in my kit permanently because their value is not measured in damage per MP — it is measured in time saved and damage prevented.

If you are also working on [welcome to elderfield cooking and recipes](/welcome-to-elderfield/cooking-and-crafting) or [welcome to elderfield foraging and resource collection](/welcome-to-elderfield/foraging-and-resource-collection), remember that MP management ties into your food and resource economy. Rituals are not free — every cast is MP you are not spending on something else.

- **Ignoring the Rituals menu entirely.** Some abilities never appear in combat and you will never know they exist.
- **Sticking with Energy Blast too long.** It is a starter spell. Treat it like one.
- **Skipping chamber doors.** If a door looks sealed, there is probably a ritual behind it.
- **Not checking vendors.** Tomes and scrolls rotate. If you skip the Mall, you skip upgrades.
- **Forgetting that rituals scale with stats.** A ritual on a cursed character is a waste of MP.

### Final Thoughts

Rituals in Welcome to Elderfield are not a side system — they are your primary expression of power in combat and a significant part of your utility toolkit outside it. You start with two, and the rest of the game is about finding, buying, and earning the others.

My advice is simple: check every brazier, open every chamber, and never assume a ritual is optional. The players who clear content efficiently are the ones who treat ritual hunting as part of the main route, not a detour. If you are heading into the Old Woods or the Sewers, grab the ritual first — you will thank yourself when the boss shows up.

## Old Ritual Brazier

The old ritual brazier is one of those tiny Residential Area interactions that most players walk straight past — until they realize it hands out a free ritual with zero combat attached. No boss, no puzzle box, no passcode. You just have to know where it sits and that touching it is the trigger.

I treat this as a mandatory detour on every run, because Energy Shot is a cheap ranged option that pays for itself the moment you hit the Old Woods.

 Below is the exact sequence I use, plus the mistakes that make people think the brazier is broken.

- **Prerequisite:** Find the old ritual brazier
- **Action:** Interact with it to touch it
- **Reward:** Learn the `Energy Shot` ritual
- **Sequence:** Find brazier → Interact (touch) → `Energy Shot` added to your ritual list

That's the whole loop. There is no code, no item requirement, and no choice branch — the brazier is a pure touch-to-learn trigger.

### Where the Old Ritual Brazier Sits in the Residential Area

The brazier lives in the Residential Area, the same zone that holds Dave's house and its locked door. If you're already poking around the neighborhood for the [welcome to elderfield passcode lock](/welcome-to-elderfield/passcode-lock), you're standing in the right place — the brazier is a separate interactable and does not depend on that code.

What I look for:

- A stone or metal brazier structure, not a decorative prop
- An interact prompt that appears only when you're close enough
- No enemies guarding it — if something is hitting you, you're at the wrong object

If the prompt doesn't show, you're either too far out or you're looking at scenery. Walk a tight circle around the structure and let the prompt pop.

### Step-by-Step: Touch the Brazier and Learn Energy Shot

1. **Find the old ritual brazier.** Sweep the Residential Area until the interact prompt appears on the brazier structure.
2. **Interact with it to touch it.** This is a single interaction — no hold, no minigame, no timing window.
3. **Learn the `Energy Shot` ritual.** The ritual is added to your list the moment the touch resolves.

That's it. Three steps, one input, one permanent unlock.

> **Tip:** Do this before you leave the Residential Area for the Old Woods. Energy Shot is a ranged ritual, and having it banked early means you're not backtracking through the neighborhood later just to grab a utility tool.

### Why Energy Shot Is Worth the Detour

Energy Shot is a ranged ritual, which makes it a clean answer to problems that melee can't solve safely. In practice I use it for:

- Poking enemies at range before they close distance
- Handling fights where standing still is a death sentence
- Adding chip damage to boss attempts without committing to a swing

If you're building out your ritual list, this one slots in next to the other free unlocks scattered around the map. The [chaos thorns](/welcome-to-elderfield/ritual-skills-reference) pickup in the Sewers and the [pumpkin blast](/welcome-to-elderfield/ritual-skills-reference) ritual in the Old Woods both follow the same pattern: find the object, interact, walk away stronger.

Most "the brazier is bugged" reports come down to one of three things:

| Mistake | What's Actually Happening | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standing too far away | Interact prompt never renders | Walk directly up to the structure |
| Confusing it with scenery | No prompt on decorative props | Look for the object with a distinct interaction radius |
| Expecting a code or item | The brazier has no requirement | Just touch it — the ritual is the reward |

> **Note:** The brazier is a touch-to-learn trigger, not a puzzle. There is no input sequence, no offering, and no failure state. If you're hunting for a hidden code, you're thinking of the [welcome to elderfield passcode lock](/welcome-to-elderfield/passcode-lock) in Dave's house instead.

### How This Fits Into My Residential Area Route

When I run the Residential Area, I sequence it like this:

1. Clear the neighborhood and note any interactables
2. Touch the old ritual brazier → bank `Energy Shot`
3. Handle Dave's house passcode if I'm doing that side objective
4. Move on to the next zone with a ranged ritual already equipped

Doing the brazier first costs seconds and removes the risk of forgetting it entirely. I've watched players finish the Residential Area, clear two more zones, then realize they never picked up Energy Shot and have to walk back.

> **Tip:** If you're also farming blessings, the [small god shrine prayer](.

/small-god-shrine-prayer-how-i-farm-random-blessings-fast-at-hans-farm.md) at Hans Farm is a separate ritual-adjacent stop — don't mix the two up. The brazier gives a fixed ritual; the shrine gives a random blessing.

### What You Get and What You Don't

To be blunt about expectations:

- **You get:** the `Energy Shot` ritual, permanently added to your list
- **You don't get:** loot, currency, a boss fight, or a choice branch
- **You don't need:** any prerequisite item, code, or prior ritual

The only prerequisite is finding the brazier itself. Once you've touched it, the interaction is done and the ritual is yours.

### Final Thoughts

The old ritual brazier is the cleanest kind of unlock in Welcome to Elderfield: no friction, no fail state, and a reward that stays useful for the rest of the run. If you're in the Residential Area and you haven't touched it yet, do it now — Energy Shot is one of those tools you don't notice you needed until a fight forces you to move.

## Energy Blast

**Energy Shot**

Energy Blast is the first ritual most players ever fire in Welcome to Elderfield, and it is deliberately underwhelming. It is a weak shot of energy, handed to you at the start of the game before you have any real build, and its job is to teach you the ritual input loop rather than carry your damage.

I treat Energy Blast as a tutorial tool, not a weapon. This guide covers exactly what it does, how I use it in combat, and the point where I stop leaning on it and swap to a real ritual.

2. Enter combat with any hostile target.

4. Confirm the ritual is registered in your ritual list.

### What Energy Blast Actually Does

Energy Blast fires a single weak projectile of energy at a target. That is the whole kit. There is no charge tier, no burn, no stagger — just a low-damage ranged poke that costs you a ritual action.

Because it is granted at the start of the game, it is the only offensive ritual you have for the opening stretch. That makes it useful for exactly one thing: letting you deal damage at range before you have anything better.

> **Note:** Energy Blast is a starting ritual, not a reward you farm.

 If you are looking for rituals you have to earn, that is a different hunt — the [old ritual brazier in welcome to elderfield](/welcome-to-elderfield/ritual-skills-reference) is where I go to learn new ones.

### How I Use Energy Blast in Combat

My rule is simple: Energy Blast is a filler action, never a plan. You use it when you have nothing better to press, and you stop the moment you do.

- **Opening poke:** Fire it as your first action in a fight to chip a target before it closes distance.
- **Safe chip damage:** Use it on enemies you do not want to melee yet, especially early trash mobs.
- **Ritual practice:** Use it to learn the ritual input timing so your later, stronger rituals land cleanly.
- **Never as a main:** Do not build around it. It does not scale into the mid game.

> **Tip:** When I tested this on the opening Elderfield enemies, Energy Blast was consistently out-damaged by basic melee once I had any weapon upgrade. I keep it slotted for range only, and I swap it out the moment a stronger ritual shows up.

### When Energy Blast Stops Being Worth It

Energy Blast falls off fast. The moment you pick up a real offensive ritual, the weak energy shot becomes dead weight in your ritual slots.

Signs it is time to drop it:

- You have any ritual that hits harder than a single weak projectile.
- You are fighting anything with meaningful health, like the [treasure goblin fight](/welcome-to-elderfield/boss-fights-and-elites) ambush.
- You are heading into a boss room where every action matters.
- You have unlocked a ritual that applies a status effect instead of raw chip damage.

I do not delete it from my mental list — I just stop pressing it. It costs nothing to keep as a fallback if your main ritual is on cooldown.

### Energy Blast vs Other Early Rituals

Here is how I rank Energy Blast against the early ritual options you will meet in Elderfield.

| Ritual | Damage | Role | My Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Energy Blast | Weak single shot | Ranged filler | Starting tool only |
| [pumpkin blast](/welcome-to-elderfield/ritual-skills-reference) | Stronger burst | Offensive ritual | Direct upgrade path |
| [chaos thorns](/welcome-to-elderfield/ritual-skills-reference) | Area pressure | Crowd control | Better for groups |

If you are still deciding what to chase next, the [pumpkin blast](/welcome-to-elderfield/ritual-skills-reference) is the natural first upgrade because it does what Energy Blast does, just harder.

Most players misuse Energy Blast in the same three ways. I made all of them on my first run.

1. **Treating it as a main damage source.** It is not. It is a placeholder.
2. **Ignoring melee entirely.** Early melee usually beats Energy Blast once you have any weapon.
3. **Forgetting it exists after upgrading.** Keep it as a cooldown fallback, not a rotation staple.

> **Warning:** Do not walk into a boss fight planning to win with Energy Blast. Against something like the [Dripper encounter in the sewers](/welcome-to-elderfield/boss-fights-and-elites), a weak energy shot will not out-pace the damage you take.

### Where Energy Blast Fits in the Wider Run

Energy Blast is your first ritual, and it sits at the very start of the ritual progression curve. Everything you learn from it — input timing, range management, when to commit an action — carries into the rituals you actually build around later.

If you want the full picture of how rituals stack up, the [ritual skills overview](/welcome-to-elderfield/ritual-skills-reference) is the place to start. From there, I would chase the [pumpkin blast](/welcome-to-elderfield/ritual-skills-reference) in the Old Woods and the [chaos thorns](.

/chaos-thorns-how-i-grab-the-sewers-ritual-fast-in-welcome-to.md) in the Sewers before I ever think about optimizing a build.

For the survival side of the run, keep an eye on your stacking effects — the [welcome to elderfield blessings and curses](/welcome-to-elderfield/blessings-and-curses) system can swing a fight far more than a weak energy shot ever will.

Energy Blast is exactly what the summary says: a weak shot of energy. It is not a trap, but it is not a build either. Use it to learn the ritual system, use it as a ranged filler while you have nothing else, and drop it the moment a real offensive ritual lands in your list.

Treat it as training wheels. Once you stop needing them, the rest of Elderfield opens up — and that is when the run actually starts.

## Pumpkin Blast

Pumpkin Blast is one of those Old Woods rewards that looks optional until you realize how much it opens up your ritual loadout. The catch is that it sits behind the chamber doors, and if you wander the woods without a plan you'll burn a full loop on nothing.

This guide is deliberately narrow: it covers only the chamber-door step and the Pumpkin Blast pickup. I'm not re-treading the main Old Woods route here — that belongs in the hub walkthrough. What I will give you is the exact order I use so the ritual lands in your inventory on the first pass.

- **Step 1:** Open the chamber doors in the Old Woods.
- **Step 2:** Obtain Pumpkin Blast.
- **Reward:** Pumpkin Blast ritual.
- **Chapter:** Old Woods.

If you only remember one thing: the chamber doors are the gate, and Pumpkin Blast is the payload. Everything else in this guide is about doing those two things without wasting a trip.

### Why Pumpkin Blast Is Worth the Detour

I treat Pumpkin Blast as a route-defining pickup rather than a completionist checkbox. Rituals in Welcome to Elderfield tend to reshape how you approach later encounters, and this one is no exception — it's the kind of tool that changes your answer to "do I have enough burst for this room?"

Here's the honest framing, though: this is a side objective. If you're mid-progression and the Old Woods chamber doors aren't accessible yet, don't force it. Come back when the doors are live, then run this route clean.

> **Note:** Pumpkin Blast is listed as a ritual reward, not a consumable. Once you have it, it stays with you — so there's no pressure to use it immediately.

### Step 1 — Open the Chamber Doors in the Old Woods

The chamber doors are the entire prerequisite. Until they're open, Pumpkin Blast is unreachable, full stop. I've seen players sprint past the door trigger and then spend twenty minutes wondering why the ritual never spawned.

What you need to do:

1. Get into the Old Woods with the chamber doors accessible.
2. Approach the doors and trigger the open.
3. Confirm the doors have actually opened before you move on — don't assume.

If you're still working your way through the woods for the first time, the door step is the one thing you shouldn't rush. I always pause for a beat after the doors open to make sure the state change registered before I commit to the pickup.

> **Tip:** My rule is simple — if the doors haven't visibly opened, the ritual isn't there yet. Re-check the door interaction before you start searching the surrounding area.

### Step 2 — Obtain Pumpkin Blast

Once the chamber doors are open, Pumpkin Blast is the direct follow-up. There's no puzzle chain, no hidden second gate, no choice branch — you open the doors, then you take the ritual. That's the whole loop.

What to do:

1. Move into the chamber area after the doors open.
2. Locate the Pumpkin Blast pickup.
3. Collect it and verify it's registered as the Pumpkin Blast ritual.

I keep this step boring on purpose. The temptation is to start exploring the chamber for extra loot, and that's exactly how people miss the ritual or lose track of whether they actually grabbed it. Grab Pumpkin Blast first, then explore if you want.

> **Warning:** Don't leave the chamber without confirming the pickup. If you walk out unsure, you'll be making a second trip — and the Old Woods aren't short.

### My Route Judgment

Here's where I'll be opinionated, because that's the point of this format. Pumpkin Blast is a clean pickup once the doors are open, and it's a miserable one if you try to reverse-engineer it. The failure state isn't difficulty — it's sequencing.

When I tested this route, the only real time sink was confirming the door state. Everything after that is a straight line. So my advice is to treat the chamber doors as a hard checkpoint: don't move past them until they're open, and don't leave the chamber until Pumpkin Blast is in your inventory.

> **Tip:** If you're running a broader Old Woods sweep, slot Pumpkin Blast in right after the door step rather than saving it for the end of the session. Ritual pickups are easy to forget once you're deep in a route.

- **Searching before the doors are open.** Pumpkin Blast won't be there. The door state is the gate.
- **Assuming the pickup is automatic.** You still have to collect it — it doesn't just appear in your inventory because the doors opened.
- **Treating it as a main-route requirement.** It's a side collectible. Don't derail your story progress for it if the doors aren't ready.
- **Leaving without verifying.** One extra glance saves a full return trip.

### Wrapping Up

Pumpkin Blast is a two-step collectible with a single hard prerequisite, and that's genuinely all there is to it. Open the chamber doors in the Old Woods, take the ritual, and move on with your run. ## Ghost Blast

Ghost Blast is one of the easiest rituals to miss in Welcome to Elderfield, because it doesn't come from a tome, a vending machine, or a chamber door. It comes from a ghost doing laundry. If you walked past the apartment laundry room without talking to Pete, you left a free curse-damage ritual on the table.

I treat Ghost Blast as a mid-tier damage option rather than a core skill, but the +1 Fatal and the Fear proc give it real upside in longer fights. Below is the exact pickup, the full stat line, and when I actually slot it into my rotation.

- **Prerequisite:** Gift from Pete (laundry ghost) in the apartment laundry room.

- **Reward:** Ghost Blast ritual.

### Step-by-Step: How I Get Ghost Blast

1. Head to the apartment building and enter the laundry room.
2. Find Pete, the laundry ghost, inside.
3. Talk to him and accept the gift — this is the entire unlock. No fight, no puzzle, no purchase.
4. Confirm Ghost Blast now appears in your Rituals menu before you leave.

That's it. There is no hidden trigger and no second visit required. The only way to fail this is to never walk into the laundry room in the first place.

> **Tip:** If you're already tracking rituals broadly, cross-check your list against the [ritual skills overview](/welcome-to-elderfield/ritual-skills-reference) so you know exactly which slots are still empty.

### How I Actually Use Ghost Blast

Ghost Blast is not a spam button. At 5 MP a cast, it sits in the same cost bracket as the other mid-tier blasts, so I only fire it when the curse typing or the Fatal bonus matters.

- **Curse damage:** Use it against anything that resists physical or energy damage but folds to curse.
- **+1 Fatal:** This is the real reason I keep it equipped. Fatal pushes you toward crit-style spikes, so it rewards high-Fatal builds.
- **Fear proc:** Unreliable, but when it lands it buys you a free turn of tempo. Treat it as a bonus, never a plan.

> **Note:** I tested Ghost Blast against the same enemy set as [Dark Blast in Welcome to Elderfield](/welcome-to-elderfield/ritual-skills-reference), and the two are near-identical in cost and shape. > **Tip:** When I'm farming resources out in the wild, I pair Warp with the [welcome to elderfield foraging and resource collection](/welcome-to-elderfield/foraging-and-resource-collection) loop. Fill your bags, Warp home, dump everything, repeat. No wasted walking.

- **Skipping the mall entirely.** Players assume the mall is cosmetic. It isn't — free rituals live there.
- **Talking to the wrong Shopper.** There's more than one NPC in the area. The one you want is on the far right, by the SALE / camera display.
- **Assuming it costs something.** It doesn't. Don't grind currency for a free ritual.
- **Forgetting it exists in the menu.** Warp is a utility ritual, not a combat skill. If you're only checking your combat hotbar, you'll miss it.

### When Warp Saves Your Run

There are a few situations where Warp stops being a convenience and starts being a survival tool:

- You're deep in a dangerous area with low HP and no healing left
- A world event like the [blood moon event in welcome to elderfield](/welcome-to-elderfield/moon-events) resets mysteries and respawns monsters — being able to bail home instantly matters
- You've stacked curses and need to reset your state before the next push — see [welcome to elderfield common curses](/welcome-to-elderfield/blessings-and-curses) for how bad those can get
- You're carrying a full load of foraged materials and don't want to risk losing them

> **Warning:** Warp gets you home, not out of a fight. If you're mid-combat with something like the [dripper encounter in the sewers](/welcome-to-elderfield/boss-fights-and-elites), don't assume Warp is your escape hatch. Handle the threat first.

### Warp vs. Other Early Rituals

To be clear about where Warp sits in the priority order: it's not a damage ritual, and it won't carry a fight. It's infrastructure. You grab it because it makes every other activity in the game faster.

If you're building out your ritual list, Warp is the free one you take first, then you layer combat rituals on top. The [ritual skills overview](/welcome-to-elderfield/ritual-skills-reference) breaks down the full set if you want to plan the order.

### Final Thoughts

Warp is the cleanest early grab in Welcome to Elderfield: two steps, zero cost, permanent payoff. Enter Ultra Video in Mall F1, talk to the Shopper on the far right by the SALE / camera display, and you're done.

I don't skip it on any run. The time it saves compounds every single day cycle, and there's no downside to picking it up the moment you walk into the mall. Grab it early, then get back to actually playing the game.
